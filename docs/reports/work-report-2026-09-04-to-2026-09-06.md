# POS work report — 4–6 September 2026

## Executive Summary

- **A real sales-reporting defect was confirmed and fixed.** The restaurant's
  cashier report cache omitted 114 paid orders totaling **8,208,000 UZS**. The
  backend recorded payment without advancing the timestamp used to refresh
  reports, allowing old unpaid copies to remain in the cache.
- **Alpha POS Desktop 1.0.43 was built and published, and the matching cloud fix
  was deployed.** The installer, signed update, and deployed server passed the
  recorded verification checks. Four release commits across three repositories
  were pushed and verified under **MythicalCosmic** on GitHub.
- **Restaurant installation and reconciliation remain pending.** The desktop
  update still needs installation, the historical cache needs refreshing, and
  a complete closing period needs comparison against actual cash plus card and
  provider totals. The investigation establishes a reporting fault; it does
  not establish missing cash or explain every reported physical variance.

## The investigation traced omitted sales to stale report entries

**The payment ledger and the cashier's reporting database could disagree even
when payment was saved correctly.** The installed backend was Alpha POS Desktop
1.0.41, and the separately installed cashier app was Smart POS 0.0.15. The
cashier app's packaged code and its SQLite report cache were inspected through
the live support connection. The cache copy passed its integrity check, and
source hashes were checked before and after transfer.

The failure sequence was reproduced:

1. Smart POS cached an order while it was unpaid.
2. Checkout saved the payment and paid state, but a partial backend save left
   the order's `updated_at` unchanged.
3. The cashier refresh saw no newer timestamp and skipped fetching the order.
4. The sales report continued to omit that paid sale.

Direct live checks confirmed **114 stale paid states**, totaling **8,208,000 UZS**.
An offline replay of the installed refresh logic skipped all 114 records.
Advancing the timestamp in that replay caused all 114 to refresh. This mechanism
does not depend on cancellation or deletion. The amount is the value of paid
sales omitted from the cache across the inspected history, not a three-day loss
or a proven shortage of physical money.

Two business-date comparisons show the reporting impact. All figures are UZS;
the difference is the cached report total minus the payment ledger total.

| Business date | Cached report | Payment ledger | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 September | 18,168,000 | 19,144,000 | −976,000 |
| 4 September | 14,525,000 | 14,984,000 | −459,000 |

The offline refresh removed the compared cache/ledger differences from
30 July through 4 September. The first, partially captured date, 29 July,
retained a separate **132,000 UZS** coverage gap. Historical recovery has not
yet been executed on the restaurant computer.
See the cache comparison (`reports/sales-live-evidence-2026-09-05/cache_summary.json`, project evidence)
and installed-code replay (`reports/sales-live-evidence-2026-09-05/22_installed_cashier_reproduction.json`, project evidence).

**Broader ledger checks narrowed the investigation.** Local and cloud daily
paid sales matched across all **66 completed dates from 1 July through
4 September**. Within the aligned comparison scope, **34,083 order money
records**, **33,970 positive payment rows**, and **64,810 item rows** matched.
The item-sum and bill-total checks found no arithmetic mismatch across
**34,437 paid orders in each database**. These are internal consistency checks,
not independent evidence of the money physically collected.
See the ledger comparison summary (`reports/sales-live-evidence-2026-09-05/summary.json`, project evidence).

The investigation also documented creation-date versus payment-date report
differences on **46 of 66 dates**, differing closing windows, and a historical
**127,000 UZS** shift-repair inconsistency. These were recorded without changing
report definitions or rewriting historical ledger figures. They remain relevant
when comparing reports that appear too high as well as too low.
See the full investigation (`SALES_DISCREPANCY_INVESTIGATION.md`, project evidence).

## Additional fixes were prepared but remain outside the release

The code investigation also reproduced and patched three areas in the original
working checkouts:

- Concurrent cloud checkout and order edits could overwrite a settled order's
  total or paid state. The prepared fix locks the order before editing and
  restricts metadata saves to the edited fields.
- Fractional checkout rounding could make a collected amount differ from the
  stored bill. The prepared fix preserves the stored amount when no new
  discount is requested.
- The read-only tender audit was extended to detect more payment/header
  inconsistencies and produce branch-scoped JSON evidence.

These changes are **uncommitted and undeployed**. Their failure scenarios were
reproduced in tests, but the saved live checks did not establish them as the
cause of the restaurant's reported variance. The older
code-audit report (`reports/sales-integrity-audit-2026-09-05.md`, project evidence) documents this
phase; its earlier “no release” status is superseded by the 6 September release
record for the timestamp and desktop shift-lock fixes.
